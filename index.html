How to read it

Watch the constriction. The yellow marker shows where the airway is narrowest — lips for /p/, the ridge behind your teeth for /t/, the soft palate for /k/. That location is the consonant.

Vowels differ by three things you can see: how high the tongue body sits, how far forward it is, and whether the lips round. Compare /iː/ (high, front, spread) with /uː/ (high, back, rounded) and /ɑː/ (low, back, open) — the meters below the model make the difference numeric.
